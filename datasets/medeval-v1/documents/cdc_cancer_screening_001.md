# Cancer Screening Tests

Source: CDC, "Cancer Screening Tests." This MedEval seed document is a concise
attribution summary for evaluation development, not medical advice.

CDC says screening tests can find breast, cervical, colorectal, and lung cancers
early. The page explains that screening means checking the body for cancer
before symptoms appear, and that regular screening may find these cancers early
when treatment is likely to work best.

CDC says it supports screening for breast, cervical, colorectal, and lung
cancers as recommended by the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force.

For breast cancer, CDC describes mammograms as the best way for many women to
find breast cancer early, before it is big enough to feel or cause symptoms.
For cervical cancer, CDC says the HPV test and the Pap test can help prevent
cervical cancer or find it early.

For colorectal cancer, CDC says colorectal cancer almost always develops from
precancerous polyps in the colon or rectum. Screening tests can find
precancerous polyps so they can be removed before they turn into cancer, and
can also find colorectal cancer early when treatment works best.
