# Preventing Heart Disease

Source: CDC, "Preventing Heart Disease." This MedEval seed document is a
concise attribution summary for evaluation development, not medical advice.

CDC says heart disease prevention starts with healthy lifestyle choices and
managing health conditions. The page says a healthy lifestyle can help keep
blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar levels normal and lower the risk
for heart disease and heart attack.

CDC describes healthy food choices as eating plenty of fresh fruits and
vegetables, eating fewer processed foods, limiting saturated fat and trans fat,
limiting salt, limiting sugar, and drinking less alcohol. It also says physical
activity can help maintain a healthy weight and lower blood pressure, blood
cholesterol, and blood sugar levels.

The source says cigarette smoking greatly increases the risk for heart disease.
If a person does not smoke, CDC says not to start; if a person does smoke,
quitting will lower the risk for heart disease.

CDC also says people with high cholesterol, high blood pressure, or diabetes can
take steps to lower their risk for heart disease. It advises people taking
medicine for high cholesterol, high blood pressure, or diabetes to follow a
clinician's instructions and not stop medicine without first talking to a
doctor, nurse, or pharmacist.
