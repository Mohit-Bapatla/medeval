# Vaccine Recommendations Before, During, and After Pregnancy

Source: CDC, "Vaccine Recommendations Before, During, and After Pregnancy."
This MedEval seed document is a concise attribution summary for evaluation
development, not medical advice.

CDC says getting recommended vaccines before or while pregnant helps protect
both the pregnant person and the baby from potentially serious diseases. The
page says a pregnant woman should get vaccinated against whooping cough, flu,
COVID-19, and respiratory syncytial virus, also called RSV.

For whooping cough, CDC says vaccination during pregnancy helps the body create
protective antibodies and pass some of them to the baby before birth. CDC
recommends getting a whooping cough vaccine during the 27th through 36th week
of each pregnancy, preferably during the earlier part of that time period.

For flu, CDC says pregnant women are more likely to have severe illness from
flu, possibly because of changes in immune, heart, and lung functions during
pregnancy. The page tells pregnant women to receive a yearly flu vaccine.
